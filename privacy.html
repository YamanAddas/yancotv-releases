Privacy policy

Last updated: 19 September 2026

YancoTV is a media player. It plays content from playlists you configure. There are no accounts, no analytics, and no servers of ours in the middle.

What the app stores

Everything the app knows lives on your device: your playlist sources, channel lists and programme guide, favourites, watch history, resume points, recordings and settings. Provider usernames and passwords are stored in your device's hardware keystore, not in plain text. Nothing is uploaded to us — we have nowhere to upload it to.

What the app sends, and to whom

  • Your IPTV provider or providers. The app contacts only the provider URLs you add, to fetch playlists, guide data and streams. Your credentials go to your provider — that is how IPTV login works. The only other place a stream address goes is a device you send it to yourself; see below.
  • Crash reports, which are on by default. When the app crashes, freezes or hits an internal error, a report goes to Sentry so it can be fixed: stack traces from the app's threads, the recent log lines leading up to it, your device model, and the operating-system and app versions. No playlists, no viewing history, no credentials — a provider address has both your login and the provider's hostname removed before anything leaves the device. Turn "Crash reports" off in Settings → About and nothing is sent.
  • Update checks. The app periodically fetches a small version file, and the new APK from GitHub when you accept an update. That tells GitHub your IP address, as any download does.
  • Subtitle searches, when you run one. The title you are searching for goes to OpenSubtitles. Nothing else does, and only when you ask for subtitles.
  • Another device on your own network, when you send something to it. Casting to a Chromecast, or using Play on TV, hands that device the address of what you are watching so it can play it — and with some providers that address contains your login. On a television the app also listens on your local network (port 8731) so your phone can find it and send it something, and announces itself there under the device's name. Only a device that knows the pairing code shown in Settings can send it anything.
  • Your television itself. If its search or its voice assistant asks installed apps for results, YancoTV can answer with matching titles and artwork from your own lists. The app can also add a row of recently watched titles to the television's home screen, which means the television's launcher holds those titles and fetches their artwork from your provider. What you say to an assistant is handled by your television's maker, not by us.

What we never do

No ads. No trackers. No analytics SDK. No selling or sharing of data. No reading of anything beyond what the features above need.

Your data, your device

Uninstalling the app removes its own database and settings. Two things are kept outside it on purpose and stay behind: recordings, which by default are written to the device's own Movies/YancoTV folder so that any video app can see them, and backups you export, which go to Downloads/YancoTV. Other apps on the device can read both. Protect a backup with a password when exporting, because an unprotected backup file contains your provider logins in readable form.

This website

This site is static and served by GitHub Pages. It sets no cookies and runs no analytics. Your language choice is kept in your browser's own storage and never leaves your device. GitHub logs requests to its servers, as any web host does, and the download button sends you to GitHub.

Changes

The copy of this policy inside the app and the copy on this page are the same document, and they change together. When they change, the date at the top moves.
