Privacy policy for iPhone and iPad

Last updated: 17 November 2026

YancoTV is a media player for IPTV services you already subscribe to. It has no user accounts and no advertising, and it does not track you. This policy explains what the app keeps on your device, what it sends, and who receives it.

What stays on your device

Your playlist sources, channel lists, programme guide, favourites, viewing history, resume positions, recordings, downloads and settings are stored only on this device. Provider usernames and passwords are encrypted with a key held in the iOS Keychain. We do not receive any of this.

So that Siri and Search can find your titles, the app adds their names and categories, and descriptions of the ones you watch, to this device's Spotlight index. Titles you have locked with your PIN are left out. The index stays on the device and is removed when you delete the app.

The app also keeps technical logs on this device to help diagnose problems. They go to no one unless you share them yourself from Settings → About. They name channels, titles and provider addresses, and what a voice request was heard as, so read them before you send them. Usernames and passwords are removed from them as they are written.

What the app sends

Your IPTV provider. The app connects only to the provider addresses you enter, to fetch playlists, guide data and streams. Your login details go to your provider, because that is how its service signs you in.

Crash and diagnostic reports. These are on unless you turn them off in Settings → Privacy → Crash reports. When the app crashes, freezes or hits an internal error, it sends a report to Sentry, the service we use to find and fix faults. A report contains the technical details of the fault, recent entries from the app's technical log, your device model, and the iOS and app versions. The log entries can include the name you gave a source. The app also sends a short record each time it is opened and closed, carrying a random number created when the app was installed, so that we can tell how often it fails. Reports never contain your provider's address, username or password, which the app removes before anything is sent, and never contain your viewing history or your searches. A report about a playback fault can carry the app's internal number for the channel or title involved, but not its name.

Sentry, like any server, sees the internet address a report comes from. We have set it not to store that address, but it records an approximate location (country and, often, city) worked out from the address before discarding it. Reports are used only to fix the app.

A network check. To choose the right way to play a stream, the app asks api.ipify.org, a free public service, what internet address your device appears to have, once over an ordinary connection and once over an encrypted one. This happens when the app starts, when your network changes, and when you run Settings → Network check. The request carries only what any web request does: your internet address, and the app's name and version.

Voice search. When you tap the microphone, Apple's speech recognition turns what you say into text. If this device has Apple's offline speech model for the language, that happens on the device. If it does not, the recording is sent to Apple for transcription. Nothing is recorded until you tap the button, and the app does not keep the recording. When you ask Siri for something, Siri gives the app the words it heard. In both cases Apple's own privacy policy covers what Apple does.

Casting. When you cast to a Chromecast or another Google Cast television, the app sends that device the address of what you are watching, or streams it to the device from your phone over your Wi-Fi. With some providers that address contains your login. iOS asks for your permission before the app can look for devices on your network.

What we do not do

We do not show ads, use analytics or advertising SDKs, track you across other apps or websites, or sell or share your information. We do not run any server that receives your sources, your library or what you watch.

Your choices

You can turn crash reports off at any time in Settings → Privacy. Once they are off, the app stops sending them.

Deleting the app deletes everything it stored on this device. Backups you export stay wherever you saved them. A password encrypts the source settings in a backup, but with many providers the stream addresses saved in it still contain your login in readable form, so keep backup files private.

Reports carry no name, email or account, so we usually cannot tell which ones came from you. If you have a question about them, write to us and we will help as far as we can.

Children

YancoTV is not directed at children. Parents can lock titles and hide categories with a PIN in Settings → Privacy → Parental controls.
